Help

Questions, answered

How reporting, tracking and collection work on WasteTrack.

Reporting

How do I report a waste problem?

Create a resident account, tap Report waste, choose a category, describe what you see, pin the location on the map and submit. A photo helps the desk verify faster but isn't required.

What can I report?

Overflowing public bins, illegal dumps, blocked drains, missed household or commercial collection, and scattered refuse around markets, motor parks and roadsides.

Tracking

How do I follow my report?

Every report gets a tracking code (e.g. WM-HE9U5TJ). Open My reports to see each case move through the pipeline: Submitted → Verified → Scheduled → Collected.

My report was rejected. Why?

Usually because the location couldn't be identified or the case was a duplicate. Check that the pin sits close to the site and add a landmark people in Bauchi will know, then submit again.

Collection

How long does collection take?

It depends on urgency and scheduling. High-urgency cases are prioritised; nearby verified reports are grouped into efficient collection runs. Your case's status always shows where it stands.

Who collects the waste?

Collection officers working with the Bauchi State Environmental Protection Agency (BASEPA) handle scheduled runs across the metropolis.

Still stuck?

If the problem is urgent — a blocked drain during the rains, waste blocking a road — report it with High urgency so the desk sees it first.
